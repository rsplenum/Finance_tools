## 1. Rahul's two answers (recorded; D-POL-12, D-POL-13)

**1. The cash flow sets the loan (D-POL-12).** The loan a business can carry is what its cash available for the loan can repay over the term, at the loan's rate, with the lender's cover kept. That figure comes first. The loan asked is then read against it:
- it carries the loan: say so;
- it carries less: show what closes the gap, each on its own and together: a longer term, running at a higher use of the plant (more sales), a smaller loan with the promoters bringing more;
- nothing within the limits closes it: say the project cannot repay this loan at the promoters' present share, and what would have to be true.

**2. Interest in the repayment holiday, two ways (D-POL-13).**
- **Added to the loan**: each month's interest of the holiday is added to the principal at that month's end; the instalments are then worked on the larger balance.
- **Paid as it falls due**: someone must have the money, since the project is not yet selling. A **new unit** has no sales to pay it from: the promoters pay it from their own sources, and the page says how much they must keep ready. An **expansion** of a running business is taken to pay it from the cash it already earns.
Either way the interest up to the first sale is part of the project's cost.

Today's engine does only the second way, for a new unit: `engine/loan.ts` `months` keeps the balance level through the holiday and takes the interest as paid; `engine/report.ts` puts the interest before the first sale in the project's cost (`idc`) and the promoters' share funds it. D-POL-02 ("interest … paid; no principal in the moratorium") stays the stated assumption; D-POL-13 adds the other way and the expansion's source.

**Rahul's example, worked two ways** (the engine's `schedule` and the annuity's closed form agree to the rupee; an illustration only: the 10% rate and the equal monthly instalments are ours, the 1.25 is `engine/data/ratios.json`'s lowest-year level, no tax or holiday): sales of Rs. 1 crore leaving 10%, so Rs. 10,00,000 a year for the loan. At 1.25 the instalments may take Rs. 8,00,000 a year (Rs. 66,667 a month). That repays a loan of:

| Repaid over | The loan it carries |
|---|---|
| 5 years | Rs. 31,37,691 |
| 7 years | Rs. 40,15,778 |
| 10 years | Rs. 50,44,744 |
| 15 years (the longest in our data) | Rs. 62,03,829 |
| however long | never above Rs. 80,00,000: the interest alone on that takes the whole Rs. 8,00,000 |

So Rs. 1 crore is beyond any term: about Rs. 50 lakh at 10 years, as Rahul said, and the rest must come from more sales (about Rs. 16.1 lakh a year for the loan would carry Rs. 1 crore over 15 years) or more of the promoters' money. This is the page we do not have.

## 3. What holds us back

### 3.1 The UI and UX
1. **The page asks for a plan and then judges it.** A borrower types 33 boxes, the loan among them, and on step 5 learns pass or fail. Their real question is the other way round: how much can my business carry, and how do I get to what I need? Rahul's first answer is that question. No screen answers it, and the engine has no function for it on the report (`viable.ts` finds a "largest loan", aimed at all eight ratios, and shows it as one fix among many). Every round since OR1 has rearranged the same form.
2. **Eight checks stand as equals.** "3 of 8" sends the borrower after eight things. One is the limit (can the cash repay it); one is a lever (the promoters' share); the rest are a bank's follow-on checks. The screen has no order of importance because the model has none.
3. **Each new screen grew its own fixer.** Eight modules search around a judge by trial; none starts from the cash flow. FIX1's search is the best of them, and it still finds plans by stepping a grid, because nothing tells it how far the cash can go.
4. **The screens are forms; nothing is handled.** Type, Next, read. A page that feels expert lets a person move a thing and watch the answer move: drag the term and see the loan it carries grow. Our levers sit nine screens down behind "Adjust it yourself", and charts are drawn by hand one at a time.
5. **A page is welded to its checks and its saved shape.** 184 check lines hang on ids; saved answers drop if a field is added (`docs/UX-OVERHAUL.md` §8: "add none in these rows"); four tools hold four state models (`dscr/model.ts` 951 lines, `report/model.ts` 1,157). A bold change costs a rewrite of the checks, so changes come small.
6. **We design without looking, for a reader who is an expert.** Rule 5 prefers text counts to pictures, so a screen is judged by its word count. The one reviewer is a banker; the customer is a first-time borrower. 342 decisions and 26 reviews have gone by with no outside user, so "intuitive" has had no test.

### 3.2 Features that would make a borrower's life easier
1. **The borrower must know what they came to find out.** We ask the use of the plant, variable costs as a share of sales, days of stock. Rule 14 (never default a missing fact) has been read as "ask everything". A sourced figure the borrower accepts or overtypes is not a default: starting shapes by trade are in PLAN's list and in no row.
2. **The reading and the drafting are built and switched off.** `POST /read` and `POST /draft` on the Worker answer "not switched on" until the key is set. So no quotation or statement is read into the boxes and no narrative is drafted: the two things that save a borrower most typing.
3. **Nothing carries over.** Answers live in one browser, one tool at a time; each tool asks the same facts again. A consultant, who would pay for many files, cannot keep one. FILE1 is row 8.
4. **The paper is thin where a lender reads hardest.** Nine pages, no "what if" rows, no chart, no account of what the business can carry (REPORT2, row 7).
5. **The loan is one draw on one day, with one way to treat the holiday's interest.** No added interest, no difference between a new unit and an expansion in who pays it (Rahul's second answer).
6. **The lender's levels are placeholders.** `ratios.json` and `solve.json` are "illustrative": "right on the first submission" needs them confirmed by Rahul or a lender, level by level.
7. **The row's bookkeeping takes the row.** Three of the last four L rows reworked one page; each ends in docs, counts and a tracker. The time went to the form and its records, not to the model under it.

## 4. The hardest problem, and the overhaul

**The hardest problem: turn the product round.** From "type your plan and we judge it" to "tell us what the business earns; we say what loan it can carry and shape the deal with you until it fits". It is hard because it is the engine's question, the page's first screen, the result's layout and the paper's first page all at once; and it is the one change that makes the rest simple.

The rules of the overhaul (every row):
1. **The cash flow comes first.** The first figure on any result is the loan the business can carry, beside the loan asked. Everything else explains or closes the gap.
2. **One limit, one lever, then the rest.** Can it repay (the limit). The promoters' share (the lever). The bank's other checks sit third, in one list, with the professional's depth in a drawer.
3. **The engine answers before a screen is drawn.** A screen shows only what a function in `engine/` already returns. No new fixer: `engine/capacity.ts` says how far the cash goes and what closes the gap; `engine/solve.ts` stays the one combiner; `fix`, `viable`, `plan` and `work` retire as their screens go.
4. **Handle it, don't fill it.** The term, the use, the promoters' money and the holiday are handles on the result screen; the bar moves as they move.
5. **An answer in the first minute.** Three or four boxes give a rough figure, marked rough with what is still assumed (rule 14: provisional, never defaulted); each further answer firms it. No box is added to any default path (rule 17).
6. **Look at what is built.** A design row ends with its new screens seen on a phone and a desktop, at most six pictures; a rebuilt page's check lines hold on words, roles and figures, not ids.
7. **Five outside people before another round of polish.**

Kept from UX2 (`docs/UX-OVERHAUL.md` §2): one job a screen, say it once, the engine solves and the borrower picks, every red line has a button, plain words first, the paper is the reward.

### 5.1 CAP1
`engine/capacity.ts` (new, pure; reuses `projectReport`, `diagnose`, `viable.ts`'s smaller-loan change, `solve.ts`'s limits; nothing worked twice):
```ts
export type CloserId = 'term' | 'use' | 'price' | 'own' | 'holiday';
export interface Closer { id: CloserId; from: number; to?: number; closes: boolean; reaches: number; input?: ReportInput }  // alone, the rest as typed; `reaches`: the loan carried at its limit
export interface Carry {
  asked: number; carries: number; gap: number;                         // rupees; gap 0 when it carries
  limitedBy: { check: 'dscrLowest' | 'dscrAverage' | 'cash'; fy: string };
  how: { fy: string; available: number; level: number; forInstalments: number };   // the tightest year: the cash, the level, the cash ÷ the level
  closers: Closer[]; verdict: 'carries' | 'closable' | 'notAsItStands';
  also: RatioCheck[];                                                   // the bank's other checks at the loan asked
  report: Report;
}
export function carry(x: ReportInput, o?: { levels?: Levels }): Carry | Needs | Blocked;
```
- `carries`: the largest term loan, the project's cost held and the promoters bringing the rest, at which the lowest-year and the average DSCR meet their levels and cash never runs short. By bisection to the box's rounding (D-POL-02: keep it monotone). It aims at the cash only; `also` reports the other ratios, never bends the figure.
- `closers`, each alone: `term` (more instalments, the whole term within `LEVELS.term.longestMonths`), `use` (to `salesLimit`), `price` and `holiday` (within `engine/data/solve.json`), `own` (the gap itself, with the promoters' share was → becomes). One that cannot close says how far it reaches.
- `verdict`: `closable` when a closer closes alone or `solve` returns a plan that works; else `notAsItStands`, with the nearest.
- On the DSCR page `maxLoanAmount` already gives the figure: the same sentence there, no new box.
- Tests (`tests/capacity.test.ts`; the `money-maths-checks` skill): at `carries` a fresh run meets the levels, and one rounding step above misses; the table in §1 as a loan-only case (schedule against closed form); a public worked example of loan sizing with its source; the sim's invariants (a higher rate never carries more; a longer term never less); a case that carries returns no closers; the same input, the same answer.
- As built (D-POL-14): `limitedBy` adds `cost` (a larger loan cannot be worked at all), `nearest` and `keepReady` (cash short whatever the loan); a closer's `reaches` is the loan carried at the least change that closes, not at its limit; `engine/fix.ts` stays, `engine/work.ts` uses it.
- The least page: above the verdict on the Check step, "Your business can carry a loan of Rs. …. You asked Rs. …", one bar, the closers as lines each with its button (applied as typed text, undone in one tap, as `applySolution`); the sticky bar's line becomes "Carries Rs. … of Rs. …". Delete `engine/fix.ts` and its test if still unused.

### 5.2 IDC1
- `LoanTerms.holidayInterest?: 'paid' | 'added'`; absent means paid, so every golden case stands. `months`: when added, a holiday month's interest joins the balance at the month's end and nothing is paid; the instalments are worked on the balance at the holiday's end. Second computation: the balance there is the amount × (1 + r)^months.

## 7. Traps
- DSCR is judged a financial year at a time and income is never shared by months (D-POL-05): capacity is read on the report's years, not on a monthly cash model.
- Never hardcode a level: the 1.25 and the 1.50 are `ratios.json`'s, illustrative, and a borrower may change them.
- `carries` moves the loan with the cost held, so the promoters' share moves: say what they bring, as FIX1's plans do.
- A closer writes typed text into boxes: round as the box shows and run again (`docs/UX-OVERHAUL.md` §8).
- The tax depends on the interest, so no closed form gives `carries` on a full report; the closed form checks the loan-only case and the explanation line uses the engine's own year.
- §1's table is an illustration for Rahul, never a figure for a page.
