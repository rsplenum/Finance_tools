# Brand names and trademarks (D-BIZ-03)
Checked on 05-10-2026 from the public sources listed at the end; not legal advice. Before the pSEO pages are indexed, an Indian IP lawyer reviews this file, the page templates and the notice once (`docs/OWNER.md`).

## In short
The estimate may name brands such as Asian Paints, Kajaria or Jaquar to point to their own products. Indian law allows it when the use is honest, takes no more of the mark than needed and suggests no link with the brand. The cases lost turned on more than naming: prominence, logos and photos, the brand in meta-tags, ad keywords or the domain, or a suggested sponsorship. Wrong or undated prices are the other risk. No wording stops a notice being sent; these rules keep the use within what the law protects and make a notice easy to answer.

## The law
- **Infringement, Trade Marks Act 1999, s. 29:** use of a registered mark in the course of trade, which includes advertising (s. 29(6)(d)); page titles, meta-tags and search-ad keywords count. A mark with a reputation in India is also protected against use that takes unfair advantage of it or harms it, with no confusion needed (ss. 29(4), 29(8)). Suggesting a link with a brand can be passing off, registered or not.
- **What is allowed, s. 30:** using a mark to identify the owner's own goods, if honest and without unfair advantage (s. 30(1)); saying what a product fits, where reasonably necessary and with no implied connection (s. 30(2)(d)). The Delhi High Court's three tests for nominative fair use (the GeM case, 2022): the product cannot readily be named without the mark; only as much of it is used as needed; nothing suggests sponsorship or endorsement.
- **Where users of a mark lost:** "Suitable for HAWKINS Pressure Cookers" with HAWKINS picked out in red used more of the mark than needed (Hawkins v. Murugan, Delhi HC, 2012). A site that used a brand's name in its meta-tags, with its images, took an active part and was liable (Christian Louboutin v. Nakul Bajaj, Delhi HC, 2018). A brand sold as a search-ad keyword is use in advertising, infringing where it confuses, even at first glance (Google v. DRS Logistics, Delhi HC Division Bench, 2023). Domain names are protected like marks (Satyam Infoway v. Sifynet, Supreme Court, 2004).
- **Comparisons:** honest, checkable comparisons, price included, are lawful; running a rival down is not (Havells v. Amritanshu Khaitan, Delhi HC, 2015).
- **Consumer law:** under the CCPA's 2022 guidelines on misleading advertisements, a disclaimer may not hide material facts or correct a misleading claim, and is in the same language and type as the claim; the penalty is up to Rs. 10 lakh, Rs. 50 lakh for a repeat (Consumer Protection Act 2019, s. 21).
- **Copyright:** the brands' photos, shade cards and catalogue text are protected; a single price is a fact.
- **Not law:** Google's scaled content abuse policy (March 2024) ranks down pages made in bulk mainly to rank.

## The rules
Everywhere brands show (the estimate, its downloads, the pSEO pages):
1. Brand names in plain text only: no logos, product photos, shade cards, brand fonts or colours, and never bigger or bolder than the item's name.
2. The generic item name first; the brand after it, as an example, or in brackets when the line is that product.
3. A price beside a brand comes from its source and date; an item named after one product is checked against a page, not "as reported".
4. This notice, word for word, wherever brands show, in the same type as the rest:
   > Brand names belong to their owners and are used here only to name their products. We are not linked to, paid by or endorsed by any of them. Each price comes from the source and date shown; where brands are listed as examples, the price is for the level, not a quote for any one brand. Prices change: check with a seller before you buy.
   Only where a brand shows (owner, 05-10-2026, D-BIZ-05): a line or an open drawer that names one, or a download that prints one. Where brands show without our prices, as on the bill of quantities, its first two sentences only.
5. Never "official", "authorised", "dealer", "partner" or "offer" beside a brand.
6. If a brand ever pays for a placement, or a link earns a commission, the page says so.

On the pSEO pages, also:

7. No brand in the domain, the site name, a page's address or its title: titles name the item and the city ("Interior paint cost in Pune"), and brands sit in a dated price table on the page.
8. Pages by item and city, fewer and richer, each with its calculator and the city's own rates; never one page per brand and city.
9. A brand in a page's meta-tags only if that page's table lists it.
10. No bids on brand names in search ads.
11. Comparisons only of published prices and specifications, dated and sourced; no "better", "worse" or "cheapest brand".
12. Only checked prices, never "as reported".
13. A line for brand owners: an address, and the days within which a mention is corrected or removed (the owner gives both, `docs/OWNER.md`).
14. Pages naming lenders' products follow the same rules, and none is aimed at the customers of the owner's employer bank (D-BIZ-01).

## Where the tool stands (05-10-2026)
- 231 of the library's items show brands as plain-text examples (210 brand names). 41 items carry a brand in their name, such as "Single-lever basin mixer (Jaquar Florentine)", each checked against a page; a test fails on one that is not. The three that were only as reported are generic now, the brand an example (TM1): p-head-grohe "Head shower set, 310 mm square", cp-cera "Basic fittings, another make", hw-blum "Top-grade hardware throughout".
- No logos or product images (`site/public` holds only the favicon); every price shows its source and date; the brand a user picks is printed in their downloads as their own record.
- The notice (rule 4) shows word for word under the items of each opened section of the planning estimate where a brand shows (a line's item names one, or the open drawer shows one), in the items' own type, and in Annex 1 of the estimate's PDF, Excel and Word copies where a line names a brand; the bill of quantities, which names brands but shows none of our prices, carries its first two sentences; both in the body's type (TM1, D-BIZ-04, D-BIZ-05). The site check reads it in all of them, and fails on any image besides the favicon in `site/public` or the built site. The typed quotation's downloads print only what the user typed, at their own rates, so they carry none: its price sentences would not hold there.
- No item or source beside a brand says "official", "authorised", "dealer", "partner" or "offer" (rule 5); a test fails on one (D-BIZ-05).
- Every page is noindex until launch (D-TECH-07), so none of this is public yet.
