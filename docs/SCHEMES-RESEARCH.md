GOVERNMENT-BACKED BUSINESS FINANCE IN INDIA

Central schemes, State/UT incentives, credit guarantees and project-report requirements

First-batch research inventory | Evidence cutoff: 11 October 2026

Purpose: help entrepreneurs, consultants and bank officers identify business/project finance routes for machinery, infrastructure and working capital, and understand what a bankable Detailed Project Report (DPR) should contain.

Scope includes credit-linked subsidy, capital subsidy, interest subvention, credit guarantee, refinance and project-based infrastructure grants. It excludes general welfare payments, personal consumption credit, and schemes unrelated to setting up or expanding a business.

How to read this report

GREEN = official evidence of operation or fresh activity into 2026 was found. AMBER = official portal/guidelines exist but the original scheme period or fresh intake beyond FY 2025–26 could not be confirmed. CONDITIONAL = policy is current, but the benefit depends on sector, district, investment, applicant category or notified application windows.

A “scheme” may be a subsidy, a loan, or a guarantee. Those instruments are not interchangeable: a credit guarantee protects the lender for part of eligible loss; it does not forgive the borrower’s debt or assure loan approval.

A scheme page being online is not proof that new applications are being accepted. Obtain the current operational guideline / portal status in writing from the implementing agency or bank before spending money or ordering machinery.

“DPR required” describes typical appraisal/documentation. Banks can ask for simpler business plans for small loans, and more detailed technical-economic reports for capital-heavy infrastructure.

Executive summary

Most useful starting points for ordinary new micro-enterprises: PMEGP (credit-linked margin-money subsidy) and MUDRA (collateral-free micro credit, but no general capital subsidy). For guarantee-backed larger MSME proposals, CGTMSE is a bank-facing guarantee facility, not a loan or grant.

For warehouses, cold chains and post-harvest assets: AIF (interest subvention and a credit-guarantee route, but current fresh-sanction window needs confirmation), MIDH/NHB cold-storage subsidy, AMI (status requires checking after its stated March 2026 period), and MoFPI’s PMKSY cold-chain component (fresh 2026 EOI activity was visible).

For food processing: PMFME is the key credit-linked capital subsidy route and ODOP is a product/district targeting framework, not itself a universal loan scheme. PMFME’s original programme period ended in FY 2024–25; despite live portals, fresh acceptance in FY 2026–27 must be confirmed before relying on it.

For activity-specific rural enterprises, National Livestock Mission—Entrepreneurship Development Programme (NLM-EDP) provides 50% capital subsidy within activity-specific ceilings (up to ₹50 lakh) and was discussed in a March 2026 Government release.

State policies can materially improve project economics, but they commonly reimburse eligible fixed capital investment, GST, power, interest or employment costs after investment/production. They are rarely a substitute for arranging promoter margin, term debt and working capital upfront.

PMEGP — Prime Minister’s Employment Generation Programme

Margin-money subsidy linked to bank finance: general category 15% urban / 25% rural; special categories 25% urban / 35% rural. New unit project-cost ceiling: ₹50 lakh manufacturing; ₹20 lakh services. Existing successful PMEGP/REGP/MUDRA units may qualify for a second loan up to ₹1 crore manufacturing / ₹25 lakh service/trading under specified norms.

Applicant margin generally 10% general / 5% special category. Bank term loan plus working capital; working-capital limits under guidelines (manufacturing up to 40% of project cost; service up to 60%). Subsidy is generally back-ended/margin money, not free cash paid upfront.

New micro-enterprises in eligible manufacturing and service activities; negative list and applicant conditions apply. A business/project report, machinery quotations and projected finance are required.

CGTMSE — Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises

Guarantee to the lender for an eligible MSE facility; it is not a loan, capital subsidy or borrower debt waiver. Eligible credit-facility ceiling raised to ₹10 crore from 1 April 2025; coverage percentage varies by borrower/facility/category.

Borrower still repays the full loan. Bank may take primary security over financed assets; eligible collateral/third-party guarantee may be waived in accordance with scheme/bank policy. Guarantee fee may apply; ask the lender to confirm coverage and fee in the sanction.

New or existing eligible micro/small enterprises borrowing through member lenders; lender chooses and lodges the guarantee. DPR depends on project and lender assessment.

Stand-Up India

Composite bank loan ₹10 lakh–₹1 crore for greenfield enterprise. Potential convergence of eligible Central/State schemes can lower effective margin requirement; scheme terms describe minimum borrower contribution, subject to applicable rules.

Borrower to contribute at least 10% of project cost under scheme terms; own contribution and subsidy convergence must be confirmed by bank. Repayment tenure may extend to 7 years with moratorium as per scheme/lender norms.

SC/ST and/or women entrepreneur greenfield businesses in manufacturing, services, trading and allied agriculture; entity ownership/control rules apply. Project report and quotations required.

Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF)

Interest subvention of 3% p.a. on eligible loans up to ₹2 crore for up to 7 years; eligible loan up to ₹2 crore may access guarantee under CGTMSE for MSMEs or NABSanrakshan route for qualifying FPOs. Not a capital subsidy.

Bank’s margin norms apply; lender-published AIF guidance typically specifies minimum 10% promoter contribution. Guarantee is limited to eligible projects/entities and covered portion.

Post-harvest management and community-farming assets: warehouses, silos, pack houses, sorting/grading, cold chain, logistics, primary processing and other notified assets; specific eligibility follows scheme guidelines. DPR and detailed financial projections are expected.

AMBER — the programme was active into early 2026, but the original scheme timeline runs through FY 2025–26; confirm that fresh sanctions are being accepted after 31 March 2026.

PMFME — PM Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises; ODOP linkage

Credit-linked capital subsidy: 35% of eligible project cost, max ₹10 lakh per individual micro unit; common infrastructure support may be 35% up to ₹3 crore for eligible group/institution projects, subject to component rules. Minimum beneficiary contribution generally 10%; balance via bank loan.

Subsidy links to bank-financed project and eligible asset costs. ODOP prioritisation/product linkage is important but not always an absolute exclusion of other food products under detailed guidelines.

Primarily existing micro food-processing units; new-unit eligibility is subject to ODOP/product and operative guideline conditions. Eligible SHG/FPO/cooperative/common-infrastructure proposals may qualify; plant/machinery and eligible technical civil works, not land/rent. DPR/project profile and financial projections are needed.

AMBER — official portal and MoFPI pages remain online, but the original approved period was FY 2020–21 to FY 2024–25; fresh intake for FY 2026–27 is not confirmed by the sources reviewed.

Borrower contribution and loan depend on the underlying funding programme; never treat “ODOP” alone as entitlement to grant or a bank loan.

District-identified products and associated processing/value-chain activities; verify current ODOP list and relevant state/central component.

2. How to combine these instruments without double-counting

Instrument

Treatment in the project report

Bank term loan + working capital

Finance fixed assets (term loan) and stock/receivables/operating cycle (cash credit or working-capital facility) as separate needs in the DPR.

Credit guarantee (CGTMSE / eligible guarantee trusts)

A guarantee supports the lender’s recovery risk. It does not create additional project funds, erase a borrower obligation, or necessarily mean the bank will lend without primary security over financed assets.

Capital or margin-money subsidy (PMEGP, PMFME, NLM-EDP, eligible NHB/MIDH)

Check whether subsidy is front-end, back-ended, reimbursed after assets/production, locked in a subsidy reserve, or released after joint inspection. Never show a back-ended subsidy as cash available on day one unless rules say so.

Interest subvention (AIF, J&K NCSS, selected state policies)

The borrower generally borrows from a bank and pays the contractual interest; government support lowers the net burden under rules. The subsidy is usually conditional on timely payments and formal claims.

State tax/investment incentives

Net-SGST reimbursements, electricity-duty relief, power subsidy, capital investment support and interest reimbursement may be claimed only after prescribed investment/production/filing. They should not automatically be treated as a source for initial project cost.

ODOP / clusters / common infrastructure

ODOP is a policy orientation, not itself a bankable finance instrument. Common facility centres and cluster infrastructure may use group/SPV-based grants, with a different application process from individual microenterprise finance.

4. DPR: essential chapters, contours and schedules

A useful DPR answers three questions: what exactly is being built, can it operate technically and commercially, and will cash generated by the business repay the debt? The required detail should match project size and the scheme. For a cold storage or large processing unit, official NHB checklists explicitly call for promoter profile, marketing strategy, implementation schedule, land/site map, layout plan, financial analysis, annexures and bank consent documentation.

DPR chapter

Essential contents / contours

1. Cover page, contents and project fact sheet

Project name, entity, location, sector/activity code, promoter, total cost, term loan, working capital, scheme(s), employment and target commissioning date.

2. Executive summary

One- to two-page concise business case: customer problem/demand, product/service, capacity, investment, means of finance, subsidy sought, expected sales, break-even and repayment capacity.

3. Promoter/entity profile

KYC, constitution, ownership, education/technical experience, management roles, net worth, credit history, existing borrowings, bank statements and own-contribution evidence. Scheme-specific eligibility proofs: caste/category, gender, residence, age, qualification, training, rural location, FPO/SHG registration, etc.

4. Business model, products and revenue

Products/services and product mix, pricing, capacity, how revenue is earned, customer segments, sales channels, order book/MOUs, seasonality, after-sales and related services.

5. Market / demand assessment

Target geography, addressable market and realistic capture, customer interviews/orders, local competition, competitor pricing, substitution risk, procurement/sales tie-ups, distribution/logistics and evidence for utilization assumptions. Avoid unsupported generic statements such as “high demand”.

6. Technical feasibility and process

Process flow, technology, rated and practical capacity, yield/wastage, quality standards, product specifications, machinery list with maker/model/rating/price, supplier quotations, installation, warranty, utility consumption, manpower, maintenance, spares and production schedule.

7. Site, land/building and statutory clearances

Land title/registered lease and permitted use; site map/layout; building plan; electricity/water/sewerage; fire and pollution approvals; Factory licence where applicable; FSSAI/BIS/Legal Metrology/GST/trade licences/environmental clearances as relevant. Identify approvals still pending and who is responsible.

8. Raw materials and operating plan

Sources, supplier terms, seasonal availability, storage, stock levels, inbound/outbound logistics, labour shifts, power/water needs, utilities and waste/by-product disposal.

9. Project implementation schedule

Land possession, clearances, civil work, machinery orders, delivery, installation, trial production, recruitment, market launch and subsidy/claim milestones. Include realistic lead times and contingency.

10. Detailed project cost

Head-wise estimates: land (separate; often ineligible), site development, building/civil works, plant/machinery, electrical installation, lab/quality equipment, installation/commissioning, technical fees, pre-operative expenses, contingency, initial expenses, working-capital margin and taxes. Mark every item as eligible/ineligible under each scheme; attach current quotations. Scheme subsidy may use standard costs rather than invoice value.

11. Means of finance and promoter margin

Show sources and uses balancing exactly: own equity, term loan, cash-credit/working-capital limit, eligible subsidy, other grants and unsecured/other loans. Separate funds available before disbursement from back-ended subsidy/reimbursement. Avoid counting one subsidy twice or treating a guarantee as a source of funds.

12. Working-capital assessment

Monthly/seasonal sales, raw material and finished-stock days, work-in-process, debtor days, creditor days, cash cycle, minimum cash, GST/receivable timing and operating-cycle calculation. Estimate cash-credit need separately from term loan and show the peak seasonal month.

13. Financial assumptions

Sales volume, capacity utilisation ramp-up, sale price, input prices, wastage, wages, power, repairs, freight, rent, administration, depreciation, interest, tax and inflation assumptions, with evidence and year-by-year change. Present base, downside and upside cases where risk is material.

14. Projected financial statements

Typically 5–7 years, or the loan tenure required by the lender: projected P&L, cash-flow statement, balance sheet, working-capital statement and funds-flow/source-use reconciliation. Ensure opening balances, depreciation, debt drawdown, interest, repayment, cash and balance sheet all reconcile.

15. Repayment and ratio analysis

Year-wise principal/interest amortisation, moratorium, DSCR, current ratio, debt-equity, interest coverage, break-even point, margin of safety and cash accrual. For infrastructure, add IRR/NPV and utilisation sensitivity if requested. DSCR norms are lender-specific; do not present a generic threshold as law.

16. Risk analysis, mitigations and ESG

Market and price risk, seasonality, input concentration, power/cold-chain outage, technology, construction delay, regulatory risk, promoter dependence, climate/water risk and mitigation measures. Include SWOT if useful, not as a substitute for numbers.

17. Scheme eligibility and compliance matrix

For each scheme: clause satisfied, eligible cost, beneficiary contribution, application cut-off, whether application must precede purchase/commencement, required bank sanction, guarantee fee, release/lock-in/inspection, claim deadline, no-double-subsidy conditions and agency responsible.

18. Annexures

KYC/entity registrations, Udyam, GST/PAN, net-worth and banking records, ITR/audited statements if available, quotations, supplier profiles, land/title/lease, site plans, licences/NOCs, customer LOIs/offtake agreements, raw-material evidence, promoter contribution proof, qualification/training certificates, scheme forms, CA/engineer certificates and lender formats.

5. Special requirements for warehouses and cold storage

Storage capacity, commodity mix, temperature/humidity ranges, chamber configuration, pre-cooling, ripening/CA/MA features, insulation and refrigeration design must match the relevant NHB/NCCD technical standards and eligible component definitions.

Show catchment-level production estimates, competing storage capacity, arrivals/seasonality, customer contracts, occupancy ramp-up, storage charges, handling/grading/packing revenue, power backup and refrigeration maintenance costs.

Separate project assets eligible under NHB/MIDH, AIF, AMI and PMKSY. A single cost item cannot automatically be subsidised by multiple programmes; examine convergence and non-duplication clauses.

Financial projections need utilisation ramp-up, tonnage-days, rate per unit of storage, energy costs, repair/maintenance, insurance, labour, electricity tariff, commodity shrinkage, working capital and debt service under low-occupancy scenarios.

NHB’s live checklist expressly lists the DPR, promoter profile, marketing strategy, implementation schedule, key map/boundaries, site layout, financial analysis, land documents, Pollution Control NOC where applicable, and bank consent letter.

6. Where to start and how to screen a project

Step

Action

A. Classify the project

New micro-enterprise; existing unit upgrade; manufacturing; food processing; agriculture post-harvest; cold storage/warehouse; livestock/fodder; or a state-incentive-heavy investment.

B. Build a 1-page eligibility sheet

Promoter category, age/education, rural/urban location, state/district/block, product, entity form, new/existing unit, Udyam, investment size, fixed-asset spend, working-capital need and intended commissioning date.

C. Screen subsidy first, but do not assume it

Check whether the programme is accepting fresh applications, if application must precede asset purchase, the eligible cost formula, caps and subsidy release sequence.

D. Have a bank discussability review

Ask a participating bank/DFI whether the activity is eligible, how much margin is required, term-loan and cash-credit assessment, collateral/guarantee route, subsidy claim interface, loan repayment tenor and required ratios.

E. Prepare one master DPR; create scheme-specific annexures

Keep one internally consistent cost-and-finance model, then create separate scheme checklists and calculations. The State incentive cost definition can differ from the Central scheme definition and from the bank’s appraisal cost.

F. Keep documentary proof of application and all disbursements

Retain online acknowledgements, dated quotations, invoices, payment trail, inspection reports, utilisation certificates, Udyam/GST, sanction letters and claim acknowledgements. Never order assets before checking the scheme’s “application-before-purchase/commencement” rule.

8. Research limitations and filing cautions

This is a wide first-batch screening inventory, not a guarantee that every State district-level scheme, sector-specific grant, current Expression of Interest or bank circular has been captured. State schemes often have narrow eligibility and periodically amended Government Orders.

Where fresh scheme intake after 31 March 2026 could not be confirmed, the entry is explicitly marked AMBER. The practical next step is to obtain the current circular/portal status from the implementing agency or the bank before drafting a subsidy-dependent financial model.

Subsidy percentages are not always applied to total project cost. They often apply only to eligible fixed capital investment or scheme-standard costs, after excluding land, working capital, used equipment, tax components, ineligible civil works or related-party purchases.

Government guarantee cover is between the guarantee trust and lender. It does not eliminate borrower liability or automatically make a project bankable. Lenders still assess promoter capability, credit history, viability, margin and cash generation.

This report is research support, not an approval, legal opinion, loan sanction or assurance of subsidy eligibility. Final reliance must be on the current scheme guideline, bank sanction terms and implementing-agency written confirmation.
