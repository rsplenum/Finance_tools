# Rahul's own words

Read this before building anything Rahul will look at. These are Rahul's corrections and directions, quoted, each with the id to grep in `docs/DECISIONS.md` for the full story. They are the best guide to what "better" means here. They guide; they are not a checklist to pass.

## The lens

- "We are creators, not imitators … It is so simple to be difficult but so difficult to be simple." Simple to use, not simple to build: the hard work is ours, never the user's. Take what exists and make it our own, or reimagine it. (D-TECH-75)
- "we need to make it our own not a replica". Take principles from a reference, never its look. (D-UX-68, D-UX-357)

## Ambition

- "I ask you to make things better and you come up with a minor iterative attempt with a list of tradeoffs like what we have is so sacred". Build the bold version for real; what exists is a draft. (D-UX-307)
- "Lessons learned supposed to make you better, they have become your chains". "Stop asking Claude to improve what it has built. Ask it to build what you originally wanted, even if that means throwing away what it has built". (D-UX-309)
- "the rules are a floor, not the film". (D-UX-325)
- "update the gate rules so they enable rather than add friction". (D-TECH-109)
- "be audacious and intuitive". The question behind Make it work: what makes this project unviable, and which levers can be pulled. (D-UX-50)
- "err on the side of creativity". (D-DATA-12)
- "you just spruced up the Hollywood hacker". Asked for a new thing, Rahul got the old one polished. Start from the reference's visual language, not today's frame with effects added. (D-UX-122)
- "We created them for thinking innovative design and they produced a home page design full of redundancies and visual noise. Need to review their worth and work". Said of the design agents: a team that works from rules makes noise. (D-UX-116)

## Quality is never traded away

- "Never trade completeness or accuracy for speed: the edge is all three together". (D-UX-110)
- "I want no compromises in calculations". (D-DOC-07)
- "the presentation needs working not curtailing the calculation". When a paper is too long, fix how it is shown; keep the working. (D-DOC-04)
- "why are we compromising quality? How large is the size 160kb with 5g". Quality is not cut to fit a cap we set ourselves. (D-UX-122)
- "Don't compromise on quality and creativity and aesthetics while trimming". (D-UX-73)
- "no compromise on the look and feel of the site. The limits can be reasonably adapted". (D-UX-68)
- "Avoid the avoidable. No compromise on quality". (D-TECH-36)
- Correctness first: a question the report needs goes behind a stated assumption; it is never dropped. (D-UX-225)

## What a page should do

- "Our goal is to enable the user to gather what they need quickly", "not that we spoon feed them each figure one by one". (D-UX-71)
- "beauty should be married with function". Beauty has a use of its own. (D-UX-71)
- Sophistication is earned, not dressed up: a calm order of information, small words that say what follows, complexity opened a step at a time, visuals that read as proof, and a page that never makes the user feel stupid or rushed. Never darker gradients, elaborate motion or finance theatre. (D-UX-167)
- "the less we ask a user how to fix it, the better". Give whole options that work; the user picks. (D-UX-251)
- "The 'make it work' doesn't work on project report". The engine solves and offers whole plans that pass; the borrower picks, never combines. (D-UX-346)
- "a long long vertical scroll and so jam packed", "a mind bender". One job a screen; say it once; every red line has a button. (D-UX-347)
- The borrower's real question is how much the business can carry. Answer that first. (D-UX-353)
- "Avoid avoidable repetition: it clutters". What one place shows, another in view beside it does not repeat. (D-UX-237)
- "I don't want replication on homepage". (D-UX-114)
- Every element says something, or goes. (D-UX-98)
- "hiding it behind tabs defeats the whole purpose". (D-UX-78)
- "Major design overreach spotted". A showcase is "a glimpse of what is our offering like a trailer not a saga". "Don't be verbose in the glimpse". (D-UX-76)
- "I don't even notice any improvements in design". A design change is one Rahul sees at a glance: show it beside what it replaces. (UX2b, 13-10-2026)
- "the UI/UX is as much the product as the reports". (D-BIZ-27)

## The papers

- "extremely professional and polished, no childish or unnecessary items". (D-UX-333, D-DOC-17)
- "where the related word or info not displayed what is the need for disclaimer". A notice shows only where the thing it is about shows. (D-BIZ-05)

## Films (the studio's, kept here because they hold for a page too)

- "Don't kill the cool stuff, it adds a coolness factor." "the whole point of video is coolness". "this video lacks emotions". A tradeoff between plain and cool goes to cool. A rule that every element must have a purpose, taken too far, stripped the life out, and Rahul put it back the same day. (D-UX-261)
- "Do it tastefully and in a subtle way that lands". (D-UX-330)
- Too fast to follow is a fault: give reading time. A scene fits one phone screen. (D-UX-255, D-UX-259)
- A film builds a mental model, shows a chosen few things and leaves the viewer curious; never simplistic. (D-TECH-106)

## The business, and working with Rahul

- "we have a prototype, not the final product". The UI and UX come first; every timeline is off. (D-BIZ-24)
- "So domain, legal, terms are on me. Rest is on you". Ask Rahul only what is truly theirs. (D-BIZ-25)
- "ultimately it's our custom engineering and features that is going to increase our throughput and acquire customers". (D-BIZ-17)
- "you try to access them and you fail every time and you waste my tokens". Name blocked hosts before the work. (D-TECH-35)
- "the cost is killing me". Tokens go to building and to checking that it works. (D-UX-349)
- The two links Rahul sent with the deep dive showed the depth of research to match; they were read as design references. Say what you take each link for, and cover all of what a deep dive names. (D-UX-357, 11-11-2026)

## The one lesson under all of these

Nine times every check passed and Rahul rejected the result (the home page's look, the trailers, the Hollywood hacker, the film that looked like slides, the mind bender). Passing checks is not the result. The result is what Rahul, and then a real borrower, sees and can do.
