# Working on this repo (for AI agents and developers)

1. Use simple language to communicate with Rahul and be articulate and to the point so that Rahul can make decisions. Rahul is the owner: every "the owner" in this repo means Rahul (D-TECH-98).
2. Outcome before architecture (Rahul, D-UX-307, D-UX-309): we are creators, not imitators (D-TECH-75). Start from the reference-quality result, never from what we have built; if the machinery cannot produce it, replace the machinery: sunk cost is not a requirement. For a look, a page, a flow or a film, build the boldest version that serves the user; scale back only on Rahul's word. Lessons and past decisions inform, never bind: when one stands in the way of the result, set it aside and say so in a line. Simple for the user, ambitious in the work, sophisticated behind it where needed. Small is only for the questions a user answers (rule 17).
3. Recommend, don't survey (Rahul, D-TECH-98): say in a line or two what you will do and why, then do it. Name a tradeoff only when it could flip Rahul's decision, at most two, each with your pick. Ask only what is truly Rahul's (money, law, customers, the name); never hand over a list to sort.
4. Start with `docs/HANDOFF.md` (the state and the Next table), grep `docs/GOTCHAS.md` for the files you will touch, then only the parts of `docs/DECISIONS.md` you need (indexed). `docs/PROJECT.md` has the what/why; read §1 and §8 of `docs/LESSONS-CARRIED.md` once per session.
5. Keep token use low (D-TECH-23): grep before reading, text assertions over screenshots, independent commands in one step, many pages through one script that prints only what is needed. End each session by HANDOFF's list.
6. One deliverable per session, with a budget set before it starts (D-TECH-29): its row in `docs/HANDOFF.md` names what Rahul will open, its size (S, M or L) and its plan; preparing the next row is part of the session that ends. The deliverable is the real thing, rough but real (the page clickable, the film moving with its sound), never a step toward it: no plan, stills, frames or tooling first (D-UX-307). Stop at the budget plus a quarter and hand off the rest; `npm run cost` gives the actual. A quick ask may stay while the cache is warm. On a cost guard warning (D-TECH-32) tell Rahul once and follow their word. A file every session reads whole stays short, capped by a check.
7. Sessions (D-TECH-62, D-TECH-70): one row at a time, each in a fresh session that pushes its row's branch first: the claim. A branch and its PR are its maker's; another session only reads and reports until Rahul hands it over. A hook guards both. A row writes the shared docs itself: no notes, no fold.
8. Gears (D-TECH-26): start at the effort the row names, in the app's words: high, or Extra where the main work is engine maths or design; Max is for bursts Rahul switches on (a skill cannot change it, E2). Think briefly on docs, bookkeeping and regenerated files, fully on money maths, a red check or a design choice. Keep the model the session started on. Hand an errand that reads much and returns little to the `fetcher` agent; never a figure, engine code, a design or a fix. Never take a figure from WebFetch (a small model's summary). Ultracode stays off.
9. Websites (D-TECH-35): before reading any website (curl, a browser or the `fetcher`), list its hosts, run `npm run hosts -- <hosts>` once and give Rahul the blocked ones to allow first; never try a blocked host. Still blocked: `docs/OWNER.md`.
10. Answers stand (D-TECH-39): once given, an answer is final; revisit it only when Rahul points out a problem. An error met by chance gets one line, never a review.
11. Skills in `.claude/skills/` load by their descriptions: `brief-first`, `money-maths-checks`, `lender-documents`, `lessons`. General copies in `portable/` go to Rahul's claude.ai account, and a lesson that holds anywhere goes there too (D-TECH-27).
12. Numbers come only from the deterministic engine (`engine/`, pure TypeScript, no DOM). Never let an AI compute amounts, ratios, eligibility or prices shown to users. AI may draft narrative text from the user's own inputs, marked as a draft, and, beside typing, read figures from the borrower's own documents, each shown with its page and line, cross-checked by the engine and confirmed by the borrower before it counts (D-UX-44).
13. Rules, benchmarks and rates live only in dated data files under `engine/data/`, each value with its source and date; lender-specific values are marked unverified until confirmed.
14. Never default a missing fact: show what is needed and keep the result provisional. The only exceptions are the assumptions in `engine/data/defaults.json` (D-UX-08) and the architect's rules in `engine/data/architect.json` (D-UX-18), each listed on the page with its reason until changed (on the planning estimate, behind one line with their count, D-UX-32).
15. Test against worked figures from outside the model: Rahul's own fictional cases worked at home, or public worked examples with their source. Never anything from Rahul's office or employer, not even anonymised (D-BIZ-02). Every quoted figure has an independent second computation.
16. Screens use plain words; lists Rahul gives are examples, not the full set; where rules are silent, flag, do not restrict.
17. A page's default path asks only the inputs Rahul listed for it: a ceiling on questions, never on ambition. For the DSCR front door: Rahul's seven inputs, who the borrower is among them, with depreciation as an optional eighth (D-UX-10). Everything else is an assumption stated on the page or sits under one closed "More options"; the site check counts the default fields and fails if the count grows (D-UX-12).
18. Every change: `npm run verify -- --quick` (0 violations; the site check on touched pages), a line per decision in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, commit; draft PR, ready when CI's full check is green (D-TECH-68). Checks guard money, law and the page contract, never taste: what Rahul sees is done when they say it is better (D-UX-307). Rahul says "merge".
19. No bank-internal material, circulars or customer data in this repo. Sample data anonymised; secrets only in environment settings.
20. Legal condition (D-BIZ-01): market across India on the internet, but never sell directly to the customers of Rahul's employer bank: no pages, campaigns or outreach aimed at them, and none of that bank's material.
21. The estimate works "as an architect would", but never calls the tool, a page or a document an architect: the Architects Act 1972, s. 37, keeps the title to registered architects (D-UX-18).
